2016 Stock Giants — Analysis

THEMES NOTICED

NVDA
NVIDIA CORPORATION — 2016
+205% YTD
In 2016 Nvidia was a gaming company, specifically for GPU manufacturing. Their GeForce cards were the industry standard. Nvidia launched CUDA in 2006 — in 2016 CUDA 8 was released alongside Pascal Architecture and the Tesla P100 (3,584 CUDA cores, HBM2 memory). NYU, UOT, and the Swiss AI Lab accelerated their neural networks on Nvidia GPUs. AI research communities had been using Nvidia products for years.
GPUAI HARDWAREGAMING
MU
MICRON TECHNOLOGY — 2016
+138% YTD
Micron makes DRAM and NAND flash. In 2015–early 2016 there was a massive price crash from overbuilding. Micron almost went bankrupt but persevered. By mid-2016 supply normalized, DRAM prices stabilized, and NAND demand surged with SSD adoption. Micron pulled back from aggressive capacity expansion — a disciplined move that set up their recovery and eventual dominance.
DRAMNANDRECOVERY
AMAT
APPLIED MATERIALS — 2016
+56% YTD
AMAT was a standout performer unlike Micron — it didn't need recovery, it was booming all year. They make the equipment required to build chips — serving all sides of the industry. Revenue split across three segments: AGS (services), semiconductors systems, and display. Consistent earnings, genuine market share gains, and ideal positioning for the chip industry's future.
CHIP EQUIPMENTB2BSTRUCTURAL
CSX
CSX CORPORATION — 2016
+56% YTD
CSX had been suffering from low commodity pricing and coal decline. CSX hauls a huge amount of freight on US eastern railroads. November 8th, 2016 — Election Day — changed everything. Trump campaigned on reviving coal, and CSX recorded its highest single-day rise: +6% among eastern railroads. CSX's surge was almost entirely dependent on that political moment.
RAILROADCOALPOLITICAL CATALYST
ULTA
ULTRA BEAUTY — 2016
+60% YTD
Ultra Beauty's success came from their all-in-one-model stores — prestigious brands alongside mass-market brands with in-store salons. No one else offered this range of cosmetic products. Their Ultamate Rewards loyalty program drove constant return customers. Critically, Amazon was not yet a major threat — in 2016 consumers rarely bought cosmetics online they'd never tried before.

2026 Stock Giants — Analysis

THEMES NOTICED

MU
MICRON TECHNOLOGY — 2026
+343% YTD
Micron became the biggest winner of the AI infrastructure wave. HBM3E (High Bandwidth Memory) became the critical bottleneck for AI accelerators — every GPU cluster needs it. Micron's capacity discipline in prior years meant they were lean and ready to scale. As hyperscalers rushed to build AI datacenters, Micron was one of the only suppliers who could deliver at volume.
HBMAI INFRADATA CENTER
PLTR
PALANTIR — 2026
+106% YTD
Palantir's AIP (Artificial Intelligence Platform) became the enterprise AI deployment layer that most large organizations couldn't build themselves. Government contracts expanded globally. The commercial segment inflected — revenue growth accelerated past 40% YoY. Palantir had spent a decade being misunderstood as a defense contractor; 2025–2026 proved it was an enterprise software company at its core.
ENTERPRISE AIGOVERNMENTAIP
AMD
AMD — 2026
+97% YTD
AMD's MI300X and MI350 accelerators took meaningful share from NVIDIA in AI training workloads — particularly at hyperscalers looking to diversify their GPU supply. ROCm software maturity improved dramatically. AMD also gained CPU share in data centers with EPYC. The dual CPU+GPU story gave AMD a rare leverage point as AI clusters required both in massive quantities.
MI300XGPUEPYC
INTC
INTEL — 2026
+85% YTD
Intel's turnaround story became credible in 2025–2026. Intel 18A process node proved competitive with TSMC N2 in early benchmarks — a major milestone after years of manufacturing delays. The CHIPS Act funding unlocked new US fab capacity. Pat Gelsinger's foundry strategy began to attract external customers. Intel went from a market darling to near-irrelevant and back — a remarkable recovery arc.
FOUNDRY18A NODECHIPS ACT
NVDA
NVIDIA — 2026
+17% YTD
NVIDIA remained dominant but faced the law of large numbers. Starting from a $3T+ market cap, even massive revenue growth produced modest percentage gains. The Blackwell architecture continued to sell out. Competition from AMD, Intel, and custom silicon (Google TPUs, Amazon Trainium) compressed NVIDIA's moat slightly. Still the undisputed leader in AI compute — but the easy money had already been made.
